# The value equation

> Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)

Every element in the ladder exists to move one of these four terms. If a
deliverable doesn't move one, it gets cut — that's the test.

## The dream outcome (numerator, maximise)

Not "learn to buy businesses." The outcome is:

> **You own a business that pays you more than your job did, you bought it
> without your own capital, and you didn't quit your job to do it.**

Stated in the buyer's own units: a business doing $500k EBITDA, acquired at 3x
on a structure where the seller and the bank fund the purchase, throwing off
$180k–$250k of distributable cash after debt service. That is the number the
price is anchored to — not the price of a course.

Push the outcome further for the two owner avatars: not one business, but the
platform for a roll-up that exits at 8–12x — the arbitrage between buying at 3x
and exiting at 9x is the real dream outcome, and almost nobody selling
"acquisition education" articulates it.

## Perceived likelihood of achievement (numerator, maximise)

This is the weakest term in this market and therefore the biggest lever.
Prospects have watched people fail at this. Things that move it, in order of
strength:

1. **Done-for-you components.** Deal flow delivered beats deal flow taught.
   Every step we perform instead of teach raises perceived likelihood more than
   any testimonial.
2. **A named, inspectable mechanism.** 3C — Capabilities, Capital, Closing — with
   the actual artifacts visible (a real term sheet, a real scorecard) rather
   than a promise of them.
3. **Third-party credibility.** Lender relationships and advisor names carry
   more weight than our own claims, because they're checkable.
4. **The guarantee.** Risk reversal is likelihood in financial form: if we're
   willing to work free until you have an accepted LOI, we're telling them the
   odds from our side of the table.
5. **Proof at their exact starting point.** A corporate exec with a mortgage
   does not believe a case study about a serial acquirer. Segment proof by ICP.

## Time delay (denominator, minimise)

Two clocks run, and both need shortening — the **perceived** first-value clock
and the **actual** outcome clock.

| Milestone | Target | How it's compressed |
|---|---|---|
| First value | Minutes after registering | Bankability Scorecard delivered on registration, not at close |
| First seller conversation | 14 days | Outreach lists and sequences built in week one, not sourced by the member |
| First structured offer | 60 days | Term sheet templates plus a live structuring session, so nobody drafts from scratch |
| Accepted LOI | 6 months | Deal desk review removes the unbankable deals early |
| Completion | 9–12 months | Pre-vetted lenders shorten the funding leg from months to weeks |

Publish these as the program's actual milestones. A timeline someone can see
themselves inside beats any adjective.

## Effort and sacrifice (denominator, minimise)

The buyer is employed, has a family, and has a mortgage. Every hour and every
dollar of theirs we remove multiplies value:

- **Zero personal capital** is the headline effort reduction — the entire
  positioning.
- **Deal flow delivered**, not "here's how to build a list."
- **Templates, not blank pages** — LOI, term sheet, engagement letters, data
  room index.
- **A weekly time budget stated up front** ("five hours a week, two of them on
  calls you schedule"). Naming the cost lowers it; leaving it vague raises it.
- **No resignation required.** The acquisition is the exit from the job — that
  sequencing removes the single biggest sacrifice in the prospect's head.

## Scored: before and after

Ten-point scale, honest assessment of a typical acquisition course versus this
architecture.

| Term | Typical course | This ladder | What did the work |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dream outcome | 8 | 9 | Roll-up exit arbitrage stated, not just "buy a business" |
| Likelihood | 3 | 8 | Done-for-you deal flow, lender access, deal-or-we-work-free |
| Time delay | 3 | 7 | Value in minutes, seller calls in 14 days, published milestones |
| Effort/sacrifice | 4 | 8 | No capital, no resignation, templates and delivered lists |

The gap between 3 and 8 on likelihood is the entire offer. Competitors sell
information; this sells the two things information can't buy — deal flow and
credibility.
