# Proof engine

Proof is the constraint on everything else. Capture is a process with an owner
and a calendar, not a favour asked after a win.

## What to capture, and when

| Moment | Artifact | Consent captured |
|---|---|---|
| Enrolment | 60-second "why I'm doing this" video, starting point stated | At enrolment |
| First owner conversation (day 14) | Screenshot of the log + one line of reaction | Standing |
| First offer submitted (day 60) | Redacted term sheet, structure only | Per-deal |
| LOI accepted | Video reaction, same day. This is the highest-value asset in the business. | Per-deal |
| Funding secured | Structure breakdown: price, vendor note, senior debt, DSCR | Per-deal |
| Completion | Full case study + on-camera interview | Written release |
| 6 and 12 months post-close | Cash flow reality, what broke, what they'd redo | Written release |

The day-14 and day-60 artifacts matter more than the completion story for cold
traffic: a prospect believes a stranger who is two weeks ahead of them more
readily than one who is two years ahead.

## Rules

1. **Redact the seller, never the structure.** The structure is the proof; the
   seller's identity is theirs, and leaking it costs a member their deal.
2. **Substantiation on file before the claim goes live.** Every number in every
   creative has a source document. This is what makes a regulator or a
   sophisticated buyer a non-event rather than a crisis.
3. **Disclose material connections** on every testimonial — incentives, fee
   waivers, affiliate arrangements. Required, and cheap to do.
4. **Never imply typical results.** Publish the distribution, not only the top
   of it. In this market the honest distribution is more persuasive than the
   outlier anyway — a buyer who does diligence for a living distrusts a page
   where everyone wins.
5. **Capture failure too.** A member whose deal fell through and who says why is
   the single most trust-building asset available, and nobody in this category
   publishes one.

## Flipping the negative

Every complaint gets a conversation within 24 hours and ends in an artifact —
a resolution, an added deliverable, or a documented parting on good terms. A
public complaint gets a public, factual, non-defensive reply and a private
resolution. What cannot be flipped is a claim that was never true; that is why
rule 2 exists.
