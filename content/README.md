# Operating principles

Extracted from the $200M marketing-lessons transcript and converted into rules
this business can be held to. The transcript is a set of claims from one
operator's experience — treated here as a strong prior, not gospel. Where a
principle collides with the reality of selling acquisition advisory to
sophisticated buyers, the adaptation is noted.

| # | Principle | Our rule | Adaptation for this market |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | People buy from people | Every asset has a named face. No faceless brand accounts, no stock-footage ads. | Advisors and members go on camera too, not just the founder — in M&A, the *bench* is the credibility |
| 2 | You are not the market | No angle ships without evidence from a real buyer conversation. | Our buyers are 35–55 with capital or careers at risk. Founder instincts from consumer info-marketing mislead here |
| 3 | The best people-readers make the best marketers | 5 buyer/prospect conversations a week, logged, non-delegable. | Run them as deal conversations, not surveys — this ICP tells you the truth in a deal context and performs in a survey |
| 4 | Six beliefs must all be installed | Every asset is tagged with the belief it installs. Gaps get filled deliberately. | See `belief-map.md` |
| 5 | Nobody cares about the product | Lead with the transformation. The mechanism is the *reason to believe*, never the hook. | Stop leading with "3C framework" or "vendor finance" — lead with owning the business |
| 6 | Every sale is emotional | Build intensity to the ask in one sitting. Don't create desire and then park it. | Then let logic justify: this buyer needs the spreadsheet *after* the decision, or they churn |
| 7 | People always come back — don't burn the lead | Nobody is ever removed from nurture for not buying. 12-month re-offer calendar. | Acquisition timelines are long; a lead who says no today may buy on their next bonus or redundancy |
| 8 | Speed beats perfection | 15 creatives a week shipped, reviewed weekly, killed on data. | Compliance review is the one gate speed doesn't get to skip |
| 9 | You need dissenting opinions | Nothing ships from the person who wrote it. A named reviewer signs off. | |
| 10 | Proof is king | Proof capture is a process with an owner, not a favour we ask after a win. | Redactable deal proof: structure and multiple, not the seller's identity |
| 11 | Any bad situation can be flipped | Every complaint gets a conversation and a resolution artifact. | |
| 12 | Marketing is the highest-leverage skill | Founder time is protected for offer, hooks and proof — the three things that can't be delegated. | |

## Two places we deliberately diverge

**Manufactured trust theatre.** The transcript's example is engineered goodwill
moments between operators. The mechanism is real and we use it — appearing
alongside lenders, advisors and members builds transferable trust. The line we
don't cross is fabricating relationships or results we don't have. In a market
where buyers do diligence for a living, invented proof isn't a risk of being
disliked, it's a risk of being disqualified permanently.

**"Edge them until they buy."** Emotional escalation to the ask is correct and
we use it. But a $9,700 program sold to someone who was manipulated past their
own judgement produces refunds, chargebacks and a member who never completes a
milestone — which breaks the conditional guarantee model the whole ladder rests
on. We build intensity, then hand them the numbers so the decision survives the
next morning.

**Compliance is not optional here.** Earnings and results claims in financial
and business-opportunity marketing are regulated (FTC in the US, ASA/CAP in the
UK, and adjacent rules where advisory work is involved). Every claim needs
substantiation on file, testimonials need disclosed material connections, and
"typical results" framing needs to be truthful. Build this into the review gate
now — retrofitting it after a complaint is far more expensive.
